How it works

Light and offline. Built with AI, not sold as AI.

How it works

  • Modular. A permanent shell (galaxies, camera, zoom) plus one small module per lesson. Add a lesson = one file and one registry line.
  • Runs offline. v1 is one HTML file (about 0.9 MB). No account, no cloud. Progress stays on the device.
  • Phone as controller. QR code on the local network; sliders and tilt drive the lab live. Hosted relay is next.
  • Handwriting, in-house. Write a number with your finger; our own template matching reads it, offline. Digits only today.

AI, honestly

  • In the product today: read-aloud voice (the browser's built-in text-to-speech). No cloud AI. No student data leaves the device.
  • In how we built it: a team of Claude agents (builder, designer, tester, reviewer) wrote and checked the code. The three of us set the goals, review every result and decide.
  • Next: a hint tutor that tells a stuck student what to try next. Only with parental consent. Assumption: state as a plan

New since round 1

  • v2, the new foundation. Shell + one module per lesson; delete any module file, nothing else breaks.
  • 5 lessons on the new modules; each port checked against v1.
  • Kept from v1: handwriting pad (Algebra); phone controller with the same QR pairing, relay and tab sync.
  • Tactile Light and Dark at AA contrast; automated contrast and lesson-flow checks.

Business model

Free to learn. Schools and parents pay.

Key partners

Teachers and school administration (pilot). No signed partners yet. Hosting provider. Assumption: no partner named

Key activities

Build lessons, pilot with classes, teacher feedback loop.

Key resources

The app and lesson modules; the team; AI-assisted build pipeline.

Value proposition

Learn by doing: 3 minute hands-on labs, instant feedback, EN and RO. Unlike PhET, Brilliant or Khan Academy, your phone controls the experiment (as far as we found).

Customer relationships

Students: free self-serve. Schools: onboarding call, class dashboard. Parents: progress ring, updates.

Channels

Students, parents: social media, teachers. Schools: direct, with a warm introduction.

Customer segments

Uses: high school students, grades 9 to 12 first.
Pays: private schools (client); parents of under-18s.

Cost structure

Hosting and relay, domain, AI tooling, lesson content time, school support (slide 18).

Revenue streams

Class licence (private schools), premium (parents). Students always free. No sponsors counted as revenue.
